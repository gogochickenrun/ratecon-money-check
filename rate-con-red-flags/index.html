Rate Con Red Flags Before You Sign

A bad Rate Con is not always obvious. The highest-risk language is often buried in instructions, deductions, paperwork rules or conditions attached to accessorial pay.

1. Fixed deductions for tracking failures

Watch for flat-dollar deductions tied to tracking apps, location services, or status updates. If tracking is required, note when it must start, when it may be turned off, and what the stated deduction is.

2. POD deadlines that are easy to miss

A document may require POD or BOL submission within a specific number of hours and through a specific email or portal. A correct POD sent to the wrong place can still create a payment problem.

3. Detention with conditions but no simple headline

The Rate Con may advertise detention but require advance notice, signed in/out times, broker approval, or a minimum wait. The conditions matter as much as the hourly rate.

4. Broad offset or chargeback language

Look for language allowing the broker to offset claims, shortages, service failures or other amounts against freight charges. Broad clauses deserve a slower read.

5. Appointment penalties

A high rate can be wiped out by late pickup or delivery deductions. Check whether appointments are fixed, FCFS, or subject to rescheduling and what the document says happens if the facility causes the delay.

6. “All-in” language

An all-in rate may mean certain accessorials are already included. Do not assume detention, extra stops, lumper or other charges remain separately billable unless the document says so.

7. Missing TONU or layover terms

If the truck is dispatched and the load cancels, or if a delay pushes delivery to the next day, missing language can leave the amount open to negotiation after the problem already happened.

Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest Rate Con red flag?

Any clause that can reduce pay but is easy to violate operationally — especially tracking, paperwork deadlines, appointment penalties and proof requirements.

Should I reject every Rate Con with deductions?

Not automatically. The question is whether the rate still works after you understand the conditions and whether you can reliably comply with them.

Can a Rate Con checker replace reading the document?

Use a checker to surface terms faster, then verify important money clauses in the actual document before relying on them.
